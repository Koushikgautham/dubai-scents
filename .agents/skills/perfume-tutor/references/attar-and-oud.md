# Attar, oud and Gulf perfumery — the insider vocabulary

Names and grading vary by region and seller; teach the ranges and the red flags, and say so when a term is used loosely in the market.

## Oud (agarwood, "agar")
- **What it is:** resinous heartwood formed when *Aquilaria* (and *Gyrinops*) trees are infected or wounded; the tree produces fragrant resin in response. **Oud oil** (Arabic *dehn al oud*) is distilled from that wood.
- **Wild vs cultivated:** wild resinated wood is rare and very expensive; most modern supply comes from **plantations with induced inoculation**. All *Aquilaria* and *Gyrinops* species are on **CITES Appendix II** — legitimate trade in natural oud needs documentation.
- **Regional styles (typical, not absolute):**
  - **Hindi / Assam oud** — animalic, "barnyard", leathery, intense (Assam is in India — a story point for Indian buyers).
  - **Cambodi (Cambodian)** — sweeter, fruity, honeyed-leathery.
  - **Malaysian / Indonesian** — woody, earthy, sometimes green.
  - **Thai (Trat)** — sweet, fruity-woody.
  - **Kyara / kinam** — the rarest, most prized grade; extremely expensive.
- **Price reality:** genuine oud oil can cost hundreds to thousands of US dollars per tola. **Most "oud" in commercial perfume oils is a synthetic or blended oud accord.** That is normal — the problem is *claiming* it's pure oud.
- **Red flags:** "100% pure oud" at a low per-kg price; no origin/distillation info; no CITES story for naturals; seller can't explain wild vs cultivated.
- **Say it like a pro:** with a supplier — "Is this a natural oud oil, a blend, or an oud accord? What's the origin and roughly what share is natural?" With a buyer — "It's an oud accord built for batch-to-batch consistency; natural oud varies wildly in price and smell."

## Indian attars (Kannauj tradition, Uttar Pradesh)
- **Deg-bhapka:** traditional hydro-distillation in copper stills (deg) with vapour condensed into a receiver (bhapka) holding **sandalwood oil** — the botanical aroma is captured *into* the sandalwood base. That is a classic "attar". Kannauj is India's attar capital — a competitor *and* a fallback contract-filling hub for us.
- **Ruh:** a pure essential distillate with no sandalwood base — e.g. **ruh gulab** (rose otto), **ruh khus** (vetiver). Expensive.
- **Mitti attar:** baked earth distilled into sandalwood — the smell of first rain (petrichor).
- **Shamama:** a complex, warm "winter" attar from many herbs, spices and resins distilled together.
- **Hina:** another warm, spicy-herbal traditional attar.
- **Majmua:** a traditional blend of several attars.
- **Choya:** smoky notes made by **dry (destructive) distillation** — e.g. **choya nakh** (from a sea-snail operculum), **choya ral** (from sal-tree resin). Used in shamama-style blends.

## Gulf / Arabic terms
- **Mukhallat:** Arabic for "mixture" — a blended perfume oil (e.g. rose + oud + amber + musk). Many Gulf "attars" sold today are mukhallats made from fragrance compounds.
- **CPO (concentrated perfume oil):** trade term for the oil concentrate — what most UAE manufacturers sell by the kg.
- **Musk styles:** **white musk** (clean synthetic musks), **Tahara musk** (very clean, soft white musk with traditional personal-care use). Real deer musk is not in legitimate trade.
- **Amber (perfumery):** a warm accord (labdanum + vanilla + benzoin), not fossil amber. **Ambergris** is the rare whale material; **ambroxan** is its synthetic stand-in.
- **Taif rose:** prized rose from Taif, Saudi Arabia — rich, spicy-rosy. "Taif rose" claims on cheap oil are a red flag.
- Classic Gulf palette: **saffron, rose, oud, amber, musk**; "rose-oud" is a signature pairing.
- **Bakhoor:** incense chips burned on charcoal — not part of our launch range.

## Units and pricing language
- **Tola ≈ 11.66 g**; attar is commonly sold in ~12 ml "tola" bottles in India and the Gulf. B2B bulk is priced per **kg**.
- Quick conversion: a per-tola price × ~86 ≈ per-kg equivalent (1000 ÷ 11.66). Use it to sanity-check margins, not as a quote.
- **Which "tola" does the shop mean?** Ask. Three readings are common, and the factor changes:
  - weight tola 11.66 g → ×85.7 (≈ ×86)
  - "12 ml tola bottle" → ×(1000 ÷ (12 × specific gravity)): SG 0.90 → ×92.6, SG 0.95 → ×87.7, SG 1.00 → ×83.3 (so ×86 is right at SG ≈ 0.97; SG is on the COA)
  - some shops round to 10 g → ×100
  - Smaller traditional units sometimes heard: masha (≈ 1/12 tola) and ratti (≈ 1/8 masha).
- Shop prices are **retail** (often including GST and possibly diluted oil) — never compare them directly with a manufacturer's bulk per-kg price for neat concentrate.

## Myths to correct
- "Attar = always natural" — most modern attars/mukhallats are compound-based.
- "Darker oil = stronger/better" — colour depends on materials, not quality.
- "Real oud never smells bad" — natural ouds can be very animalic; that's not a defect.
