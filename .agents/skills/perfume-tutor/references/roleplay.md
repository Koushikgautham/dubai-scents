# Role-play scenarios and debrief rubric

All characters are fictional composites. Play them realistically: busy, commercially sharp, sometimes evasive, testing whether the founder knows the trade. Keep in-character replies short (1–4 sentences, like real speech). Adapt details to what the user asks for.

## Scenarios

### S1 — First meeting at a UAE manufacturer (Sharjah / Ajman industrial area)
**Character:** Mr. Faisal, sales head at a mid-size perfume-oil factory; sells CPO by the kg to Gulf, African and South Asian brands. Friendly, pushes volume.
**Hidden tests:** offers "any designer type you want — Sauvage, Aventus, Baccarat"; says "we can print Made in Dubai on your labels"; quotes a price but is vague about MOQ; hesitant about formula exclusivity and escrow ("our formulas are our secret"); has SDS but IFRA certificates "on request"; has never signed an authorisation letter for Indian registration.
**The founder should:** ask for SDS/IFRA/COA and flash point; probe MOQ per oil, lead time, batch consistency, retained samples; negotiate exclusivity for India + escrow; ask about the authorisation letter; decline designer-type names and the "Made in Dubai" label politely; confirm they are the actual manufacturer (see the factory, licence).

### S2 — Souk trader in Deira
**Character:** Anwar, runs a busy perfume-oil shop; very cheap prices ("same as Lattafa, AED 300/kg"); no paperwork; offers to "send it with someone flying to Chennai".
**Hidden tests:** is a trader, not a manufacturer; no SDS/IFRA; trademark issue; suitcase shipping (illegal for commercial import without CDSCO registration).
**The founder should:** identify the trader red flags, ask who manufactures it, refuse luggage shipping, and either walk away or ask for the manufacturer's documents.

### S3 — Indian D2C founder evaluating samples (Bengaluru, video call)
**Character:** Priya, founder of a 2-year-old D2C perfume brand selling on her site and quick-commerce; currently buys oils from a Mumbai importer.
**Hidden tests:** asks "how long does it last?", "is it IFRA compliant at 20%?", "can you give me exclusivity?", "what's your MOQ and price per kg?", "why switch from my current supplier?", "are you registered with CDSCO?"
**The founder should:** avoid untested longevity numbers; explain documentation; be honest about registration status/timeline; quote terms (500 g MOQ per scent, advance payment for first orders, paid sample kit credited to first order); ask discovery questions about her pain points.

### S4 — Traditional attar shop, Chennai (T. Nagar / Triplicane)
**Character:** Rahim bhai, third-generation attar seller; prices by the tola; sceptical of "Dubai" claims; asks for "Sauvage type" and "Kashmiri oud"; wants 30 days' credit.
**Hidden tests:** tola ↔ kg arithmetic; designer-type request; credit terms; scepticism about origin.
**The founder should:** speak the tola language, offer profile-described alternatives, hold the advance-payment terms for the first orders, prove provenance with documents, respect his expertise.

### S5 — Consumer at a pop-up stall
**Character:** a 24-year-old Chennai customer used to body sprays; asks "is it halal?", "will it last all day in this heat?", "why so expensive for 6 ml?", "is it made in Dubai?"
**The founder should:** use "alcohol-free" not "halal"; explain oil vs spray performance honestly; justify price by concentration; give the honest origin line ("fragrance oils from UAE manufacturers, bottled in India").

## Debrief rubric (score each /10, then an overall)

1. **Discovery** — asked the right questions for this counterpart (documents, MOQ, exclusivity / the buyer's pain points).
2. **Accuracy** — facts and terms were correct (concentrations, flash point, tola, IFRA meaning, oud reality).
3. **Compliance-safe language** — no halal, designer-type names, untested longevity numbers, "Made in UAE", invented heritage. Any slip is called out explicitly with the safe alternative.
4. **Commercial terms** — protected the business (advance payment, MOQ, paid samples, exclusivity, escrow, authorisation letter).
5. **Rapport & confidence** — respectful, listened, didn't over-promise.

Debrief format:
```
## Debrief — <scenario>
**What went well:** …
**Missed questions:** …
**Accuracy fixes:** …
**Risky claims:** <quote> → <why> → <say instead>   (or "None — nice.")
**Better phrasing:** …
**Scores:** Discovery x/10 · Accuracy x/10 · Compliance x/10 · Terms x/10 · Rapport x/10 · Overall x/10
**Their next push-back:** "<line>" — want to try again?
```
