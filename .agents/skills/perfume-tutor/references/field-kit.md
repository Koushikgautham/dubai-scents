# Field kit — practical checks that protect the founders in real deals

Use the items that fit the lesson or scenario. Each one is something they can *do or ask* in the room, not just know.

## Documents and what to check on them
- **IFRA certificate at neat use.** IFRA certificates give a maximum use level per product category. Our roll-ons and oil-library vials sell the oil **undiluted (100%) on skin**, so the certificate must allow ~100% in the relevant leave-on skin category (commonly Category 4 for fine fragrance). A certificate that only allows, say, 20% covers a buyer making an EDP — not us wearing it neat. Ask: "What's the maximum level for Category 4, and does it cover use at 100%?"
- **SDS:** flash point (Section 9) and transport classification (Section 14) for every oil; ask for > 60 °C where possible.
- **COA per batch + retained samples:** the classic cheat is a great sample followed by a weaker production batch. Ask the supplier to keep a retained sample of every batch and to send the COA with each shipment; compare against the approved sample on arrival.
- **Authorisation letter:** can only come from the actual manufacturer; needed for the Indian import registration.

## Price maths to run on the spot
- **Tola ↔ kg:** 1 tola ≈ 11.66 g → per-kg ≈ per-tola × 86. Use it to catch "pure oud" priced like a commodity.
- **Neat vs diluted:** always compare prices **per kg of neat concentrate**. If an oil is 30% concentrate in carrier, its per-kg price must be divided by 0.3 to compare with a neat oil.
- **Cost per roll-on:** price per kg ÷ 1000 × ml (≈ g) per bottle — e.g. ₹6,000/kg oil in a 6 ml roll-on ≈ ₹36 of oil. Shows quickly what can and can't be "pure oud".
- **Volume tiers:** always ask for quotes at several volumes (e.g. 1 / 5 / 25 kg) — it reveals the real margin and the MOQ flexibility.
- **Currency:** quotes in AED; convert at the day's rate and say it's approximate.

## Before the meetings
- **Calibrate your nose:** spend an hour at two or three established oud/attar retailers (Chennai first, then Dubai) smelling a genuine natural oud next to oud accords; buy one small genuine reference. You can't judge a factory's "Cambodi" without a reference in your memory.
- **Ask "who owns the formula?" before discussing exclusivity:** if the factory bought the compound from a fragrance house, they can't grant exclusivity or escrow on it — only the formula owner can.
- **Check the trade licence:** licence holder, activity (perfume manufacturing vs trading) and emirate/free zone; most emirates and free zones have online licence lookups.
- **Bring a note-taking template:** oil code, price per kg at each tier, MOQ, documents promised, blotter impressions at 0 / 1 h / next day.

## Traps to test for
- **Payment-detail fraud:** never pay to bank details that arrive by email/WhatsApp without confirming by phone with a known contact; be suspicious of "our bank account changed".
- **Trader posing as manufacturer:** ask to see the compounding/filling floor and the trade licence; ask who their perfumers are.
- **Sample switching:** keep part of every approved sample sealed, labelled and dated.
- **"Same as <designer>" offers:** decline; it's a trademark risk for us (see claim rules).
- **Natural-material claims (oud, Taif rose, sandalwood):** for expensive naturals, a GC-MS analysis from an independent lab can confirm composition before paying natural-oil prices.
- **Pressure tactics:** "price valid today only", large deposits before samples and documents.
