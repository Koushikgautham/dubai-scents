## 1. Foundations
- **What a fragrance is:** a blend of aromatic materials (naturals + synthetics) — the blend is called the *fragrance compound*, *concentrate* or *perfume oil* (Gulf trade often says **CPO, concentrated perfume oil**).
- **Concentration** (share of compound in the finished product; ranges vary by house):
  - Extrait / Parfum ~20–40% · Eau de Parfum (EDP) ~15–20% · Eau de Toilette (EDT) ~5–15% · Eau de Cologne ~2–4% · body mist ~1–3%.
  - **Perfume oils / attars**: either the compound used neat, or diluted in a carrier (e.g. fractionated coconut oil, light liquid paraffin, IPM; DPG — dipropylene glycol — is technically a solvent, not an oil). Traditional Indian attars are distilled into a sandalwood-oil base (see attar file).
  - Alcohol-free = no ethanol carrier. Many aroma molecules are chemically "alcohols" (linalool, geraniol) — "alcohol-free" in marketing must mean no ethanol, and must be literally true for the full formula.
- **The pyramid:** top notes (first ~15–30 min, citrus/fresh), heart/middle (hours, florals/spices), base (longest, woods/resins/musks/amber). A simplification — many modern perfumes are fairly linear.
- **Families** (Michael Edwards wheel, simplified): Floral · Amber (formerly "Oriental") · Woody · Fresh (citrus, green, water, aromatic/fougère). Sub-styles: gourmand (sweet/edible), chypre (bergamot–labdanum–oakmoss), fougère (lavender–coumarin–oakmoss), leather, aquatic.
- **Accord:** several materials blended to smell like one new thing (e.g. an "amber accord" = labdanum + vanilla + benzoin).

## 2. Performance
- **Longevity:** how long it remains perceptible on skin. **Sillage:** the trail left behind. **Projection:** how far it radiates from the wearer.
- **Oils vs alcohol sprays:** ethanol evaporates fast and throws the top notes outward → strong early projection. Oils have no fast-evaporating carrier → they sit closer to the skin and release slowly; often long-lasting but quieter. This is the honest trade-off to explain to buyers who want "beast mode".
- **Climate & skin:** heat speeds evaporation; humidity can make scents feel louder while sweat and heat shorten wear; dry skin holds scent less (moisturised skin holds longer). Base notes (woods, resins, musks, amber, oud) drive longevity.
- **Testing longevity honestly:** wear tests on several people, logged at intervals (1h, 3h, 6h, 8h+), in the real climate (Chennai humidity) — required before any longevity claim (ASCI).

## 3. Materials
- **Naturals:** essential oils (distilled/pressed), absolutes (solvent-extracted, e.g. jasmine), resinoids (benzoin, labdanum), CO2 extracts.
- **Key synthetics to recognise by name:** ambroxan (ambergris-like woody-amber, backbone of many "fresh-spicy" bestsellers), Iso E Super (velvety woody), hedione (airy jasmine), calone (sea/watermelon), cashmeran (musky woody), ethyl maltol (candyfloss sweetness), vanillin, galaxolide / habanolide (clean white musks).
- **Myths:** "natural is always safer" — false; many allergens are natural (oakmoss, citrus oils). "Synthetic = cheap" — false.
- **Animal notes:** real deer musk and civet are effectively absent from legitimate trade (CITES/ethics); "musk" today means synthetic musks. Ambergris (whale) is rare; ambroxan replaces it.

## 5. B2B quality & documents (what a serious buyer or regulator will ask for)
- **SDS (Safety Data Sheet):** 16 standard sections (GHS). Read: Section 2 hazards, **Section 9 physical properties incl. flash point**, **Section 14 transport (UN number, e.g. UN1266 "perfumery products" if flammable, or "not regulated")**.
- **Flash point:** lowest temperature at which vapours can ignite. For air transport, liquids with flash point **≤ 60 °C** are typically Class 3 dangerous goods → extra cost and paperwork. Our sourcing rule: prefer oils with flash point **> 60 °C**.
- **IFRA Certificate of Conformity:** IFRA (International Fragrance Association) Standards restrict or ban certain materials. The certificate states the **maximum % the oil can be used at in each product category** under a named IFRA amendment. Ask for the certificate covering leave-on skin fragrance use and confirm the amendment is current.
- **COA (Certificate of Analysis):** per batch — appearance, colour, odour, specific gravity, refractive index, flash point. Used to check **batch consistency** (ask the supplier to keep retained samples per batch).
- **Allergen declaration:** regulated allergens present and their %. Needed for labelling and buyer trust.
- **Dosage %:** how much oil goes into a finished product (e.g. a buyer making an EDP at 18%). B2B buyers ask "what dosage do you recommend?" and "is it IFRA-compliant at that dosage?"
- **Shelf life / stability:** perfume oils typically 2–3 years stored cool and dark; maceration (resting after blending) changes the smell over days–weeks. For import, goods need ample remaining shelf life (see business plan).

## 6. Evaluating and describing scents
- Use **blotters (mouillettes)**, labelled. Smell at intervals — on dipping, 15 min, 1 h, 4 h, next day — then on skin. Don't evaluate more than ~8–10 in one sitting; rest in fresh air. "Coffee beans reset your nose" is mostly myth; smelling your own skin or fresh air works as well.
- **Describe by profile, not by brand:** family + 2–3 key notes + texture + mood. "Warm amber with saffron and a dry woody base; dense, close to skin, evening." More professional, and avoids trademark trouble.
- Comparing against a famous reference is fine **in private evaluation only**, never in naming or marketing.

## 7. Commercial basics
- **Units:** B2B bulk by the kg; Indian and Gulf attar retail often by the **tola (≈11.66 g; attar bottles commonly ~12 ml)**; small bottles in ml (3/6/12 ml roll-ons).
- **Questions every supplier conversation needs:** price per kg (and volume breaks), MOQ per oil, lead time, sample policy, batch consistency, SDS/IFRA/COA availability, flash point, shelf life, formula exclusivity for India, formula escrow, private label, export experience to India, payment terms, and willingness to sign a manufacturer authorisation letter (needed for Indian import registration).
- **Traders vs manufacturers:** many souk shops are traders reselling someone else's oils; only the actual manufacturer can give formula exclusivity and the authorisation letter.
