# Claims — what we can and can't say (researched 2026-10-18/20)

| Claim type | Risk | Rule / authority | Compliant alternative |
|---|---|---|---|
| **"Halal" / halal logo** | High | Uttar Pradesh order (Nov 2023) treated halal marks on cosmetics as falsification under the Drugs & Cosmetics Act; status should be re-checked before relying on it either way | "Alcohol-free" (only if literally true of every ingredient: no ethanol in carrier or compound) |
| **Designer names: "inspired by Sauvage", "Aventus type", "Dior-like"** | High | Trade Marks Act s.29(8) (unfair advantage / dishonest comparative advertising), passing off. Burberry v. Petrol Perfumes (Delhi HC 2024, CS(COMM) 176/2024) — injunction over names/trade dress. Counterpoint: Dior "Poison" suit dismissed with ₹2L costs (Patiala House, May 2026) — outcomes vary, but the risk for a small brand isn't worth it | Describe profile: "fresh-spicy, ambroxan-forward, peppery woods" |
| **Longevity numbers ("lasts 24 hours", "12-hour wear")** | High | ASCI Code Ch. I — claims must be substantiated; ASCI routinely upholds complaints on unsubstantiated personal-care claims | "Concentrated oil that wears close to the skin" — numbers only after documented wear tests (n, conditions, method kept on file) |
| **Projection/"beast mode", "everyone will notice"** | Medium | ASCI substantiation; oils typically project less | Honest framing: "intimate, close-to-skin" |
| **"Made in Dubai / UAE"** on India-filled goods | High | Misbranding / Legal Metrology origin; Rule 34 actual-manufacturer requirement | "Fragrance oils from UAE manufacturers, bottled in India" / label: "Made in India; fragrance oil imported from UAE" |
| **"100% natural", "pure oud", "real Taif rose"** | High unless proven | ASCI substantiation; misleading advertising (Consumer Protection Act 2019, CCPA) | Only with composition evidence (supplier spec, GC-MS); otherwise "oud accord", "rose accord" |
| **Therapeutic / health claims ("relieves stress", "cures headache", "aromatherapy for anxiety", "antibacterial")** | High | Drugs & Magic Remedies (Objectionable Advertisements) Act; a cosmetic making drug claims risks being treated as a drug | Mood/experience language: "calm, comforting evening scent" |
| **"Alcohol-free"** | Low if true | ASCI truthfulness | Keep a supplier declaration (no ethanol) on file per oil |
| **"Since 19xx", heritage, "award-winning", "No.1"** | High if untrue | ASCI truthfulness | Founder story: "two Chennai founders with family roots in Dubai" |
| **"Dermatologically tested", "hypoallergenic", "safe for sensitive skin"** | High unless tested | ASCI substantiation | Only with a test report; otherwise "patch test before use" |
| **Comparative claims vs named competitors ("better than Ajmal")** | High | ASCI comparative advertising rules; trademark | Don't name competitors |
| **Influencer posts** | Medium | ASCI influencer guidelines — disclose gifted/paid ("#gifted", "#ad") | Brief every reviewer: honest opinion + disclosure, no scripted claims |
| **Prices "lowest in India", fake discounts** | Medium | CCPA dark-pattern guidelines / Consumer Protection Act | State real MRP and real offers |
| **Indian national flag / emblem (incl. 🇮🇳 in ads, packs, logos)** | Low–medium | Emblems and Names (Prevention of Improper Use) Act 1950 restricts use of the national flag/emblem for trade or business | Drop the flag; say "India" in words |
| **Product (SKU) names copying famous fragrances ("Royal Oud", "Aventus", "Oud Wood", "Black Opium")** | High | Trademark — many fragrance names are registered marks of other houses | Original SKU names; run a quick web + IP India search per SKU name |
| **"Launching / now in India / available now" before approval** | Medium | Misleading if no stock can legally be sold (no COS-2 / loan licence yet) | "Coming soon", "join the waitlist" |

## How to rewrite
Keep the *benefit* the copy was reaching for and restate it truthfully and specifically. E.g. "Lasts 24 hours, beast mode!" → "A concentrated, alcohol-free oil — a little goes a long way, and it stays close to your skin." Avoid hollow hype; concrete sensory description sells better and is safer.
